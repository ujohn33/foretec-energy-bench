Forecast, bid, carry the imbalance.

Energy Bench runs the models itself and scores how accurate they are. The bidding competition hands the decision to you: you hold a notional wind asset on real European market data, commit a position before the gate, revise it in intraday, and settle whatever is left at the imbalance price. Every entry is scored on the money it would actually have made.

Every desk wants a proven track record, and there is nowhere to build one. Foretec editions are that venue: neutral, recurring, run in public, and open to anyone rather than to one employer.

How an edition works

  1. 01

    Qualify

    Bring your own data and models. The platform publishes the settled series every score is computed from, a baseline model that produces a valid entry out of the box, and tutorials for the free sources. A qualifying round on historical periods filters the field and opens the leaderboard.

  2. 02

    Commit, daily

    Before the day-ahead gate at 12:00 on D−1, submit a position for each of the 96 quarter-hours of the delivery day, and optionally your forecast quantiles. Revise the position once in intraday, one hour before delivery. Submissions are timestamped and locked server-side; nobody sees the outcome before committing.

  3. 03

    Settle on realised prices

    Positions settle against the published day-ahead price, the intraday index (less half the spread) and the imbalance price, with a price-impact term on the imbalance leg so that deliberate imbalance does not pay. Your score is the share of perfect-foresight revenue you captured, published at T+2 once imbalance prices are final.

  4. 04

    Rounds and the overall table

    Twelve weeks live in six two-week rounds, each with points and a prize, plus an overall table for the main prize, so one bad week does not end your edition. You compete under a pseudonym and choose when to reveal yourself; you keep your code, and only your submissions are scored.
