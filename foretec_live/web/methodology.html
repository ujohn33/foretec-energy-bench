When everything happens

The rules

  1. 01

    One task, one clock

    On each issue day D-1, every model forecasts all quarter-hours of delivery day D from data public at 11:30 CET/CEST, and the forecast is locked before the 12:00 SDAC day-ahead gate closure, exactly when a day-ahead bid would be due. A live run that finishes after the gate is never scored. The forecast covers 96 values, or 92 and 100 on clock-change days. Targets are day-ahead price (EUR/MWh), wind generation (on- plus offshore, MW), solar generation (MW) and total load (MW) for BE, NL, FR, DE-LU, DK1 and DK2.

  2. 02

    Data: ENTSO-E Transparency Platform

    Prices and actual generation come from the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform, parsed directly from its XML: day-ahead prices (document A44, the 15-minute SDAC prices), and actual generation per production type (A75), wind as onshore (B19) plus offshore (B18) and solar (B16). Quarter-hours ENTSO-E has not published are filled from the TSOs' own open data: Elia Open Data for Belgium and RTE éCO2mix for France, then Energy-Charts (Fraunhofer ISE) as a last resort; the Netherlands has no TSO service without registration. ENTSO-E alone lacked 12% of Belgian wind quarter-hours (13 of 27 days below the 95% needed to score) and 2.6% of French wind; Elia and RTE fill all of them and agree with ENTSO-E without bias (mean difference +0.2 MW for Belgian wind). Every snapshot and every set of actuals records how many points came from each source; the explorer and the Inputs table show it. The ENTSO-E day-ahead total load forecast (A65) is one of the covariates. Issue days up to and including 3 Oct 2026 used Energy-Charts only. DE-LU, DK1 and DK2 (live from 15 Oct 2026, backtested like the others) come from ENTSO-E, as Electricity Maps uses them; Danish load is published there only hourly, so DK load forecasts are scored on hourly averages of forecast and actual.

  3. 03

    No look-ahead

    Each model gets the last 28 days of history, cut off at what was public at issue time: prices through the end of D-1, which the previous day's auction published on D-2; wind and solar up to the cut-off minus one hour of publication lag. A model may also train on longer history if all of it ends before the month it forecasts: lgbm_lags and lin_lags train on the two years up to the end of the previous month and are retrained monthly, and each training row is built with exactly the history that was public at that day’s cut-off. The input snapshot is stored with every run, so each forecast can be reproduced exactly.

  4. 04

    Scored on accuracy

    Point forecasts are scored with MAE and RMSE; quantile forecasts with pinball loss over the 10th to 90th percentiles. The standings compare relative errors against the naive_weekly baseline, the same quarter-hour one week earlier, aggregating before normalising: per series (zone and target), all of a model's errors over the period are pooled first, so Rel. MAE is its total absolute error divided by the baseline's on the same days, and Rel. RMSE the square root of the ratio of squared errors; below 1 beats the baseline. When the filters select several series, these per-series values are combined by their mean, median or geometric mean, a choice the standings let you switch: the mean is moved by one series where a model is very good or very bad, the median says whether it wins on most series, and the geometric mean treats halving and doubling the error alike. The forecast explorer and the daily views use the relative error of each single day. Prices are scored on D-1 once the day-ahead auction for D has cleared, since the cleared price is the outcome a day-ahead forecast targets; it is final. Wind, solar and load are scored at 07:00 on D+1 if every quarter-hour of D has been published, otherwise at the next scoring run once 95% has; until then the explorer shows the values metered so far as provisional actuals, which are never scored. For the following three days their actuals are fetched again at every scoring run: every check is logged, and if a series has moved by more than 0.5% of its daily energy, the actuals are replaced and that day is scored again. Before 3 Oct 2026 wind and solar were scored on D+2; actuals frozen then changed by at most 0.75% afterwards.

  5. 05

    Elo score

    The standings rank models by an Elo score. A race is one issue day and one series (zone and target), and its field is every model with a scored forecast for it: a model joins the races of the series it declares from the day it is added. In each race every pair of models plays one game, won by the model with the lower error on the selected metric; equal errors split the game. All games of the selected period are fitted at once with a Bradley–Terry model (the method behind the Chatbot Arena leaderboard), which gives each model a strength such that the odds of A beating B are the ratio of their strengths. The strengths are shown on the Elo scale: 1000 is the field’s average, and a model 400 points higher is expected to win 10 games for every one it loses. Unlike a classic running Elo, the score does not depend on the order of the races, and a win over a strong model counts more than a win over a weak one. One virtual drawn game against an average model keeps the score of a model that has won every game finite. The 95% interval shows how much the score would move if the set of issue days had been different: the fit is repeated 200 times, each on the period’s issue days drawn at random with replacement, and the interval spans the middle 95% of the results. Whole days are drawn, not single races, because all races of a day share the same weather and fail together. Overlapping intervals mean the order of two models is not settled yet. A model whose run failed or was skipped is left out of that race rather than counted as a loss; the run tracker shows those failures. Models with fewer than 24 races, or half of the most any model has, are provisional and listed after the ranked ones. The standings can be rated on all races, or only on those of the last 30 or 7 days up to the latest scored issue day, to show current form; the provisional threshold then scales with the window. Best and worst day are the model’s highest and lowest share of games won on a single issue day. The zone and target filters choose which races count; the covariate filter only chooses which models are shown.

  6. 06

    Covariates, frozen at the cut-off

    Covariate models also get weather forecasts from three independent models, ICON-EU (DWD), GFS (NOAA) and IFS HRES (ECMWF), through the Open-Meteo single-runs archive. For each model only a run published before the cut-off counts (initialisation at most cut-off minus its publication delay plus a margin), which at 11:30 means the 06 UTC ICON-EU run and the 00 UTC GFS and IFS runs. Their training history uses the same rule day by day, so models train on forecasts with the same lead time as the ones they forecast with. Added: ENTSO-E's day-ahead total load forecast, which TSOs must publish two hours before the gate, and fuel cost: TTF gas (ICE Dutch front month) and EU carbon (EUA) daily closes from Yahoo Finance, a close counting only from the end of its date. Live, all of it is fetched at the cut-off and archived; in the backtest, weather runs come from the same archive, but the load forecast is today's published version, which may include later revisions. The load forecast is an input for price only. Load models have not received it since issue day 16 Oct 2026, so they compete with the grid operators' forecasts rather than learning from them. It also does not measure the same thing as every scored series: TenneT's NL forecast is net of embedded solar, while NED's NL load is gross (correlation −0.45 in Jul–Sep 2026). Live load forecasts issued up to 15 Oct 2026 used it; the backtest was re-run without it.

  7. 07

    Known caveats

    NL wind and solar are NED.nl’s national estimates (Nationaal Energie Dashboard, backed by TenneT), not the ENTSO-E series: TenneT reports to ENTSO-E only the solar plants and onshore turbines it meters itself, which put NL solar at about 1% of the fleet (20 Sep – 7 Oct 2026: capacity factor 0.1% against 10.7% for NED, 12.7% for BE, 13.6% for FR) and NL wind at mostly offshore. NL offshore wind is NED’s metered offshore output (identical to ENTSO-E’s offshore series) for delivery days from 13 Oct 2026; earlier days were scored on NED’s park-by-park offshore model, which ran 7–27% higher, and keep those scores. NED, like Elia’s Belgian solar series, upscales monitored systems to the whole fleet, rooftop PV and distribution-connected turbines included, so it is an estimate rather than a meter reading. The backtest was re-run on it; live forecasts issued up to 10 Oct 2026 had been made from the ENTSO-E series and are voided: kept and shown in the run tracker, never scored. NL load is NED’s national electricity load as well: gross consumption, including what rooftop and distribution-connected generation supply on site, as Elia and RTE report total load. The total load TenneT reports to ENTSO-E is net of that solar and drops to about 6 GW at a sunny midday against about 17 GW gross (8 Oct 2026). NED’s load history starts in 2024, so NL load climatology uses 2024 onwards; live NL load forecasts issued up to 11 Oct 2026 were made from the net series and are voided. The grid-operator benchmark for NL load is NED’s own forecast, fetched at the cut-off. The grid-operator benchmark (tso_cutoff) has no DE-LU, DK1 or DK2 wind and solar forecast: the German TSOs publish theirs on ENTSO-E only by 18:00, after the gate, and Energinet's day-ahead forecast is not yet verified as published before 11:30; their load forecasts (A65) are included. Weather points for DE-LU come from the Marktstammdatenregister, for DK1 and DK2 from Energinet's capacity per municipality (see the centroid map). Models that publish no quantiles have no pinball score and sit out rankings on pinball.

  8. 08

    Gate-aligned by construction

    The task is the decision a market participant faces, not a generic forecast. Every series is forecast for the same delivery day D, at 11:30 on D-1, from what was public at that moment: history up to its publication cut-off, the weather forecast runs already published (frozen and archived), and the grid operators' day-ahead load forecast. A run that finishes after the 12:00 day-ahead gate is not scored, and the grid operators' own forecasts as they stood before the gate are the benchmark to beat. Live benchmarks built on pre-registration, such as TS-Arena, guarantee the same absence of look-ahead differently: each series is forecast from its own last published value onwards, so the lead time follows each source's publication delay and the forecast window need not match a delivery day or a market deadline. Both are leak-free; the gate-aligned set-up also makes every forecast one that could have been bid, which is what the planned trading layer will score.

  9. 09

    Forecast value

    The Forecast Value page asks what a wind or solar forecast is worth to the producer who sells on it. The producer owns a virtual 100 MW asset in each zone that produces like the zone's whole fleet: its output is the zone's actual output times 100 MW over the installed capacity (the Marktstammdatenregister for DE-LU, Elia for BE, Energinet for DK1 and DK2, ENTSO-E's installed capacity for FR and NL), and every forecast is scaled the same way, so the score of one zone does not depend on its size and every zone counts equally across zones. Before the 12:00 gate, it sells a fixed percentile of a model's forecast (or its point forecast) on the day-ahead market, at the day-ahead price. Each quarter-hour, what it produces above that quantity is paid the long imbalance price; what it falls short is bought back at the short imbalance price (ENTSO-E Transparency Platform, imbalance prices 17.1.G; the German price is the national reBAP). The score is the revenue as a share of the perfect-foresight revenue (the actual output sold at the day-ahead price), summed over every zone and day selected. By default only the cost of the error counts: each deviation costs its size times the absolute gap between the imbalance price of its direction and the day-ahead price, so no error ever earns and the share is at most 100%. The alternative, zone settlement, settles deviations under each zone's own rules as they would be paid: separate long and short prices where the TSO publishes two (NL in the quarter-hours where TenneT activated both directions, about half of them; FR in nearly all, typically about EUR 17 apart), one price in BE, DE-LU, DK1 and DK2; then a deviation earns when the imbalance price happens to sit on its side of the day-ahead price, which over a few weeks rests on a handful of price spikes (in BE solar hours, 15 Sep to 2 Oct 2026, 2% of quarter-hours carried more than the whole average gap) and can lift a naive forecast above perfect foresight. Percentiles between the published deciles are interpolated linearly; a quantity below zero is sold as zero. The producer is a price taker: its bid moves neither price, it cannot curtail, and it does not trade intraday, so the score is what the day-ahead forecast alone earns. Only forecasts that are scored on the leaderboard count, so voided and excluded forecasts are left out here too.
