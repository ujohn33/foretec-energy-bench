Title: EnergyBench: a live, gate-aligned benchmark of day-ahead forecasts for European power markets

Authors: Evgenii Genov, Ander Martinez Alonso, Christoph Bergmeir

Abstract (1825 characters; arXiv limit 1920):
Comparisons of energy forecasting models usually rest on backtests, which are easy to get subtly wrong: the information available when a forecast would have been sold is replaced by whatever is convenient to download later. EnergyBench runs every entry live instead. Each day at 11:30 CET/CEST, thirty minutes before the day-ahead (SDAC) gate closes, every model forecasts the next day's price, wind and solar generation and load on the 15-minute grid for six bidding zones (BE, NL, FR, DE-LU, DK1, DK2). Unlike other live benchmarks, EnergyBench runs the models itself, on one information set that it freezes at the cut-off and publishes, including three numerical weather prediction models, so foundation models are compared with and without the same weather covariates under a real market deadline. Errors are pooled per series against a seasonal-naive baseline, and models are ranked by a Bradley–Terry rating on the Elo scale with a day-bootstrap interval. The field spans baselines, linear and gradient-boosted models on the frozen weather, eighteen time-series foundation models from eleven families, ensembles, and the grid operators' own forecasts. Over the first thirty live days, the covariates reduced the error of each of the ten foundation models run with and without them by 38–52% on matched races (45–52% for the seven that accept them natively; 64–72% on wind, 55–62% on solar). Used zero-shot, these models match or outperform a LightGBM model trained on the same covariates. Model size, from 3M to 331M parameters, matters far less, and median ensembles of the covariate models lead the standings. The grid operators' pre-gate forecasts remain ahead on wind and solar, where the best single foundation models have 20–28% more error; on load the two are level. A model enters the benchmark by pull request.
